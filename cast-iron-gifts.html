A new cast iron skillet does not need a second skillet. It needs the small things that keep the first one in use: something to scrape with, something to grip, a wipe of oil, and a place to set it down that is not the bare table. The reasoning behind the pan itself is in the cast iron versus nonstick guide. Smaller kitchen gifts are on the stocking-stuffers page.

Cleaning it

Lodge pan scrapers, two-pack

For the first month, when eggs stick and a sponge feels like the wrong idea. These are two small polycarbonate scrapers, red and black, with different edges for corners. The honest caveat: they are plastic. Let the pan cool before you scrape. A hot skillet will win that argument.

Lodge chainmail scrubbing pad

For the crust the plastic scraper will not lift. It is stainless chainmail around a silicone grip, made for seasoned iron. The honest caveat: it cleans. It does not dry the pan or oil it. Rinse, scrub, dry on the burner, then a thin wipe of fat. Skip the scrubber if the only problem is that nobody dries the skillet.

Holding it, and oiling it

Lodge silicone hot handle holder

For the person who has already grabbed a cast iron handle straight off the stove. Lodge lists this silicone sleeve for skillets 9 inches and larger, and it is dishwasher safe. The honest caveat: a loose sleeve is a bad way to lift a pan of hot oil. Use it as a grip, and if it slops around on a smaller handle, use a dry towel instead. The listing is for 9-inch and larger skillets, not every little pan in the drawer.

Lodge seasoning spray, 8 ounces

For the wipe of oil after washing. The bottle is 100 percent canola oil, with no propellants, in a spray.
